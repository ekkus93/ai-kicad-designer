# Supply rails drawn with supply symbols instead of long wires (2026-11-11)

The designs were generated by `NativeSchematicEngine._compile` (brief v3 power-symbol rails) from the working tree committed with this README. They were then assessed with `NativeSchematicEngine.assess` on this Linux host (KiCad 10.0.6 via `/tmp/AppDir/AppRun`), which is not the locked qualification host.

**Problem:** an outside review of inverting_gain from `review_loop_corpus_v3_2026-11-11_qwen35_256k_run3` found the drawing hard to follow. VPLUS and VMINUS ran from J1 down the left edge and along the bottom of the page to U1C. Every independent check and the qwen35-256k review had accepted it. Under brief v3, the rail planner wired every rail group it could reach, accepting routes up to 3× the direct distance. It added power symbols only for groups it could not reach.

**Changes (approved by the owner):**
- **Supply cap.** A supply rail (any asserted rail not drawn with a ground glyph) is wired between its separately drawn terminal groups only by routes up to 25.4 mm (`MAXIMUM_SUPPLY_ROUTE`). Farther groups get supply symbols. Local pairs, such as a decoupling capacitor to its pin, and power-flag paths stay wired.
- **Rerouting.** The rail planner always reroutes supply rails, so the compiler's own long runs cannot survive.
- **Return rails** (REF, GND) keep the previous rule. The repeated net labels that brief v3 forbids are not used.
- **Power unit text spot.** The text spot above an op-amp power unit is an obstacle for power symbols, so a supply arrow on pin 8 points sideways and "U1C / NE5532" stays above the pin, as the owner asked (`3479ac9f`). Only the drawn text is reserved. A padded box also ruled out sideways arrows and left VPLUS hanging downward on a stub.
