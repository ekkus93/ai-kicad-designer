////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////
///////////////     General Notes
////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

(See also file `README.md`.)

The 16 JST 2-pin connectors are a size used by many NTC suppliers. 
The NTC sensors are easily available and inexpensive.

The PCB board is 48.11mm by 70.83mm, which fits (tightly) in a standard NA electric outlet hole. 
This allows for a standard outlet faceplate cover. The board needs to be supplied by low voltage (5v).
An outlet box is not a good option because of the number of sensor wires, but a low voltage single gang 
wall plate with no back, as often used for audio or TV cable, is a good option.

The mounting hole centers are 44mm apart in x direction and 66.9 mm in y direction.

In addition to the JST connectors on the analog digital converter (adc) and the LoRa module, the board has 
  - 4 pins on an 8 pin header to connect a ST-link programming dongle.
  - 4 pins on an 8 pin header to connect ssd display on I2C1.
  - a red led indicating when it is powered up.
  - a blue led on pin PC13 which can be programmed to turn on and off.
  - a 25 MHz crystal for the high speed external (HSE) oscillator. (still untested)
  - a 32.768 kHz crystal for the low speed external (LSE) oscillator. (still untested)
  - a coin cell battery holder to supply the real time clock (RTC). (still untested)
  
The HSE, LSE, and RTC are not used by current software but are on the board for future possibilities.

//////////////////////// Sensitivity to Power Suppy

The regulator needs at least 4.3v supply on the J20 connector. Testing has been mostly done with a
5v suppy.  If a 3.2v battery is used to supply the regulator (instead of 5v) then the system boots
and the radio transmits, however the temperature readings are high by nearly 10C.

See below  `revision history and notes` for more details on temperature sensitivity to power suppy.

////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////
///////////////            LSE and HSE oscillators
////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

Current software does not require RTC battery, LSE or HSE oscillators. 
LSE and HSE are experimentation for future possibilities.

Regarding Master Clock Output on pins see 
https://community.st.com/t5/stm32-mcus/how-can-i-output-a-clock-signal-to-the-stm32-s-mco-pin/ta-p/588222
and
https://aticleworld.com/stm32h573-mco-clock-output-configuration/

From the datasheet, for UFQFPN48 chip, MCO_1 is on pa8 pin 29. 
MCO_2 requires a 64 pin package to get pc9 so some diagnostics are not available.

////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////
/////////////////////  RFM95  LoRa radio
////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

Reset pin has internal pull-up.  Do not hold low for long. Current code does not attempt reset.
See  https://www.mouser.ca/datasheet/2/975/1463993415RFM95_96_97_98W-1858106.pdf
"A manual reset of the RFM95/96/97/98(W) is possible even for applications in which VDD cannot
be physically disconnected. Pin 7 should be pulled low for a hundred microseconds, 
and then released. The user should then wait for 5 ms before using the chip."

- testing notes with v0.2.2. (Test files in crate SensorProject_t16  examples/ and src/bin/
    (5v supply actually shows 5.21v on measurement dongle and 5.14v on voltmeter.)
    - blink_default runs. Timer good on dongle and 5v supply. 
    - blink_hsi16   runs. Timer good on dongle and 5v supply.
    - blink_hse     fails. Software bug. Run time error `assertion failed: clk.raw() % FREQ == 0`.
    - text_i2c  runs (needs semihosting) using both 128x32 and 128x64 displays.
    - lora_spi_send runs on dongle and 5v. 5v supply draws 20 to 30 mA.

    - t16-f411 main bin  runs on dongle and 5v supply, with and without display. 
      With the display, the 5v supply draws 20 to 30 mA with about 140 mA spike on startup.
      Power usage is similiar without the display.
    - wire antenna on U.FL is as good as the spring coil antenna, possibly better.
    
    - measured temperature is sensitive to voltage supplied to the sensors. 
      At 30 mA the 3.3v AMS1117 regulator should be ok to about 40v supply, but the regulator
      might become noticably warm. Testing has mostly been done with 5v supplied to the AMS1117, 
      but 12v supply is also reported below.
      The AMS1117 needs dropout of 1v, so at least 4.3v supply to give 3.3v to the sensors. 
      The STlink dongle does not use the AMS1117. It supplies 3.3v directly, bypassing the AMS1117.
      If many sensors are connected then the STlink regulator may not supply proper voltage.
      Following testing was done with the equivalent of 4 sensors connected.
        J1 has NTC at room temperature (which changed during testing).
        J2 has  10K resistor connected, which should measure 25 C.
        J3 has  33K resistor connected, which should measure 0.2 C.
        J4 has 100K resistor connected, which should measure -19 C.
        J5 and others have nothing connected.

      It is also possible to power with a LFePo 3.2v battery connected directly to GND and 3v3,
      for example, using the STlink socket. Measurements below show how this affects the measurement.

      5v     XX< J1: 20.1 J2: 23.5 J3: -2.5 J4:-16.2   J5:-25.1  thru AMS1117
      STlink XX< J1: 20.7 J2: 23.5 J3: -2.5 J4:-16.2   J5:-25.1  direct  @3.2+v
      12v    XX< J1: 18.5 J2: 23.4 J3: -2.6 J4:-16.3   J5:-25.2  thru AMS1117
      LFePo  XX< J1: 21.1 J2: 24.8 J3:  0.5 J4:-13.8   J5:-22.4  direct  @3.2+v
      LFePo  XX< J1: 22.1 J2: 25.5 J3:  0.6 J4:-12.5   J5:-21.0  direct  @3.15v
      LFePo  XX< J1: 22.6 J2: 26.2 J3:  1.7 J4:-11.2   J5:-19.6  direct  @2.97v

      Note that the software calculation of the temperature from measured voltage can be improved,
      but does need to assume an adequate stable supply.
